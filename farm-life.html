Nobody here will hand you a schedule

Some guests spend three days barely leaving the verandah. Others are up before breakfast feeding the animals and out at the waterfall by mid-morning. Both are fine. I'll tell you what is possible, and then we'll see what you feel like.

Breakfast

What you will eat

Everything is cooked here, mostly from what grows on the farm. Three meals a day, afternoon tea, and refreshments whenever you want them.

If your children won't eat Thai food, tell me and I will cook something they will. Nobody has gone hungry yet.

If you'd like to learn to cook it yourself, we can do that too. It is a simple lesson in the Thai kitchen, usually a dish or two for lunch or the evening meal.

The morning

Up the valley to the waterfalls

An hour's walk up the valley brings you to small waterfalls. Bring fish food and you can watch them jump the falls, which occupies children for far longer than you would expect. You can walk back, or I'll bring the truck round and drive you back by the road if the walk has been enough.

There are gibbons in the trees near the farm. They are usually hanging around somewhere close by, and finding them is its own small expedition.

The middle of the day

It gets hot, and the farm slows down

This is the verandah part of the day. There are two lakes, an orange grove and the coffee plants to wander through, and somewhere to lie down in the shade.

Khun Nee comes over from the village if you would like a massage. She is very good.

The afternoon

Out, or not

If you want to go out, there is plenty within reach: the hot springs at San Kamphaeng where you can soak or swim, the village of Mae Kampong and its waterfall, a Karen village, the walking street markets in Chiang Mai on a Saturday or Sunday. I drive guests myself, most of the time.

Or you can stay put. The village is a short stroll away if you want a snack or a cold drink, and Khun Jean's noodle shop there does excellent noodles at lunchtime.

Evening

Dinner, and then the dark

There are no streetlights here, so the stars are the real thing. Some nights we go out looking for fireflies. In winter we light the open fire near the Thai kitchen.

With families we often make krathongs and paper lanterns, decorate them, light them, and float the krathongs on one of the lakes for safe travels and good luck. Chok dee.

There is a ruined temple on the hill opposite, a short walk away. As it gets dark we sometimes walk up to light candles and make merit.

A three-night stay, in full

This is just an example of how three nights might go. Tell me what your family enjoys and we will shape it around that.

Day one

  • We meet you in Chiang Mai — your hotel, the airport, the railway or bus station, anywhere in the city.
  • About an hour's drive to the farm. If you come by minibus we will meet you for the last stretch, because the road runs through the river.
  • Arrive, settle in, meet the animals, and walk around the farm. Lunch and refreshments as they suit you.
  • A stroll to the village for anything you need.
  • A short walk to see whether the gibbons are about. They usually are.
  • Evening meal.

Day two

  • Feed the animals before breakfast.
  • A short trek through the jungle to the waterfalls, where the fish jump the falls, then a leisurely walk back. If walking isn't for you, we will drive you there and back by road.
  • Mid-morning coffee, then a cooking lesson — a dish or two for lunch or the evening meal.
  • Lunch.
  • Free time on the farm, or a visit to a nearby Karen village.
  • After tea, a half-hour drive to the hot springs. Watch the geysers, swim, soak in a private tub, or just rest your feet.
  • Evening meal, then out looking for fireflies.

Day three

  • Feed the animals before breakfast.
  • A local tour to some of the sights nearby, including The Giant.
  • Back to the farm for lunch, then a rest through the heat.
  • Krathong making for everyone, and a craft class for the children.
  • A massage for two from Khun Nee.
  • Tea.
  • As it gets dark, a short walk to the ruined temple to light candles and make merit.
  • Back for a bonfire in winter, a barbecue, hot air lanterns, and the krathongs floated on the lake for safe travels and good luck. Chok dee.

Day four

  • Feed the animals before breakfast.
  • Depending on when you leave, there may be time for the jungle zip wire.
  • Farewells, and we drive you back to wherever you need to be in the city.

Nothing is fixedItems can be swapped, dropped or added. Most families end up doing less than this and enjoying it more.
