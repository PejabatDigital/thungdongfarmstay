Sawasdee krap. My name is Chaiwat Pankaew

Everyone calls me Ae. I think even my parents have forgotten my real name by now.

Sixteen years ago I bought a small piece of land in the hills fifty kilometres from Chiang Mai, with a river running through it, jungle around it, and good air. Then I bought the land next to it, and in the end the jungle valley too. I had no farming experience at all. I have spent every year since trying to turn it into my own idea of nirvana.

I live on the farm and I look after every part of it. It is hard work. But the farm, the animals and our guests are my joy, and I feel lucky enough to want to share it.

Ae at work on the farm — not posed. This is the most important photograph on the whole site.

What was here before

The valley was jungle once. This region was invaded by the Burmese many times, and they sacked the old temple on the hill opposite the farm. The story people tell here is that they took its golden Buddha away with them.

When we bought the land, most of it was planted with chili. We dredged the spring-fed swamps and landscaped them into two lakes.

We did not find the golden Buddha.

The ruined templeIt is still there on the hill opposite, a short walk from the farm. Some evenings we go up to light candles.

The ruined temple, or the lakes at dawn

Building the farm

The chili pickers' quarters, an old wooden building, became our farm house. Over the years we added small outbuildings for ourselves, for a few guests, and for the animals. We brought the orange grove and the coffee plants back to life and planted whatever else would grow. We helped the village build its temple on the hill nearby, which you can reach on a short walk through the jungle.

There are still no paved roads to the farm. To reach the main gate, you drive through the river.

A farm built by the people who stayed

Almost everything that has been added here came from someone who stayed. A guest from Dubai built our first website, years ago. Others have suggested ideas, helped with projects, sent their friends, and taken many of the photographs you'll see in our gallery.

We never planned it that way. It is simply how the farm has grown.
